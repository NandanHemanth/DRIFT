Dear Prof. Wang,

The DRIFT work is complete and I would like your view on where to submit it. A one-page summary is attached; the full report and code are at [repository link].

In short, DRIFT varies only the forgetting policy over a fixed memory substrate, retrieval function, answer model and judge. Across two public benchmarks, bitemporal invalidation matches append-only memory on accuracy while using 12 percent fewer tokens, novelty-gated writing raises staleness 4.5-fold (p < 0.001), and a time-blind ablation is significantly worse on both accuracy and staleness, which attributes the advantage to bitemporal semantics rather than to graph structure.

I propose two venues:

**TMLR** (<https://jmlr.org/tmlr/index.html>) is an archival JMLR-affiliated journal with rolling submissions, no deadline, and roughly four-week reviews, and it judges papers on evidence rather than novelty, which suits a controlled study.

**NAACL 2027** (<https://2027.naacl.org/>) is in San Francisco from 1 to 5 June 2027, submitted through ACL Rolling Review with the relevant cycle closing 12 October 2026, and acceptance places the paper in the ACL Anthology.

My suggestion is TMLR first, given no deadline and the faster turnaround, with the ARR submission prepared in parallel for October. I am happy to reverse that if you prefer.

One experiment remains: three of our four research questions are answered on real data, and the fourth, whether the rankings hold in multi-agent settings, is currently validated only on synthetic data. The harness is built, and the remaining work is a 21-cell run I would like to finish before we submit.

Would you have time to meet this week or next to discuss the framing and venue choice?

Finally, if the paper is accepted at NAACL 2027, could you advise on the Stevens conference travel grant, specifically graduate student eligibility, the application timeline relative to the acceptance notification, and whether faculty endorsement is required?

Thank you for your guidance.
